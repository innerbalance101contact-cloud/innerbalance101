What this stage is

You already know how this works.
Here, it goes deeper.

Same three parts, same daily practice. What changes: you name everything you're carrying instead of just one thing, using an expanded map, and the thought work goes past the surface thought into the belief actually running it.

The commitment is the same shape as before. A daily practice. 21 days. Monday to Friday, weekends optional.

Before you begin. Naming five feelings takes longer than naming one. Give each part the time it's asking for. Not the time on the clock.

When to do it

Three windows. Each one does something different.

◎
Morning

Before the day's demands arrive, while you can still tell the difference between what you're actually feeling and what you're about to perform for everyone else.

◑
Between things

The moment one task ends and before the next begins. A natural seam in the day that most people rush past. This practice is built to use it instead.

◙
Evening

Close what the day opened. You're not reviewing your to-do list. You're asking what today asked of you, and what it left behind that you're still carrying.

Before you begin: how this works

What happens between something reaching you
and how you respond to it

Something reaches you. A message, a look, a deadline, a memory. Before you've even decided anything about it, your beliefs about yourself and about the world have already filtered it. That filtered version is what becomes an emotion. The emotion shows up in your body as a feeling. The feeling produces a thought. The thought drives an action. And the action, done enough times, becomes a habit. The habit is what decides how you meet the next thing that reaches you.

Enter this cycle anywhere. That entry point is the practice.

StimulusSomething reaches you
BeliefFilters it
EmotionWhat gets generated
FeelingIn the body
ThoughtWhat you tell yourself
ActionWhat you do

Repeat the action enough times and it becomes a habit. And the habit is what decides how you meet the next thing that reaches you.

Most of that sequence runs on autopilot, faster than you can even notice it happening. Stage 3 isn't about stopping the cycle. It's about finding the interval inside it, the small gap between what reaches you and how you respond, and learning to work inside that gap on purpose.

Here's what that can actually look like, in a moment a lot of people know.

That's the whole skill. Not becoming someone who doesn't react. Becoming someone who can find the interval, look at what's actually running the cycle, and choose the next link in it on purpose. That's what self-diagnosing and self-coaching actually means, and it's what the rest of this stage trains.

In Part One, you'll use an expanded Feeling Wheel, the psychology tool for naming exactly what you're carrying, not just one feeling but everything that's actually present. In Part Two, you'll take that naming and work with both the body and the belief underneath it.

01
Part One · Feel
Body Connection
5 minutes

Sit with both feet on the floor. Close your eyes if that's comfortable. This isn't a formality. It's the foundation the rest of the practice stands on.

A · Where do you feel it?

Scan slowly from your feet to the top of your head, before you try to name anything. Notice where there is tension, weight, heat, numbness, or constriction.

  1. →Jaw, neck, shoulders. Where the load and the bracing sit.
  2. →Chest. What's tight, hollow, or heavy here?
  3. →Stomach and gut. This is often where it lives before your mind even names it.
  4. →Hips and lower back. What have you been carrying without admitting it?
B · Name everything you're carrying

In Stage 2, you used the body-feelings chart to name one emotion at a time. Now you go deeper. Tap every word on the wheel below that's true for you right now, that becomes your list for today.

Surprised Fearful Angry Disgusted Sad Happy

Tap a word to mark it. Tap again to remove it.

What you're carrying today
C · What thought is running underneath that?

Write the exact sentence your mind is running. Not the edited version, the real one. One to two lines.

Common at Stage 3: “I’ve done the work and I still don’t trust myself.” “I know what to do but I keep choosing the harder way.” “Something still feels off and I don’t know why.”

02
Part Two · Transform
Somatic Release & Deeper Thought Work
20 minutes

This is where the shift actually happens. First the body, then the belief underneath the thought. Don't skip either half. Insight without release just stays in your head. Release without insight repeats itself tomorrow.

A · Your emotions today

This is what you're working with. Want to add more? Scroll back up to the wheel in Part One before you continue.

Brought back to the six core feelings

This is what tells you which movement to use below.

B · Move it through the body

Match the practice to whatever you marked. If you marked more than one family, start with whichever has the most charge right now. Set a timer for the full time and stay in it. The nervous system shift happens in the sustained practice, not the first thirty seconds. Each one also links to a guided video, if you'd rather have someone walk you through it out loud the first few times.

Fear & Anxiety
Extended Coherence Breathing
10 minutes

Five counts in, five counts out, for the full ten minutes. Use for scared, anxious, insecure, or threatened.

Guided coherent breathing, Yoga with Sheehan
Anger & Frustration
Shaking & Discharge
10 minutes

Feet hip-width apart, knees soft, let the body vibrate gently from the ground up, increasing if it wants to. This is how the nervous system discharges what being let down, humiliated, or made furious leaves behind.

Guided TRE practice with Dr. David Berceli
Sadness & Disconnection
Qigong Flow
10 minutes

Slow arm swings, spinal waves, weight shifting with the breath. The form does not need to be exact, the intention does. Use for lonely, vulnerable, guilty, or depressed.

Guided qigong for grief, Chris Shelton Qigong
Disgust & Aversion
Cleansing Breath & Reset
10 minutes

Inhale through the nose for four counts, exhale through the mouth for eight with an audible sigh, for five minutes. Then wash your hands and face in cool water, slowly, noticing the sensation the whole time. Disgust wants something out of the body. Give it a physical exit.

Huberman Lab on the physiological sigh
Surprise & Disorientation
Grounding Through the Senses
10 minutes

Name five things you can see, four you can hear, three you can touch, two you can smell, one you can taste. Move slowly. This brings a startled or confused nervous system back into a body that is, right now, safe.

Guided 5-4-3-2-1 grounding practice
Happiness & Ease
Values-Based Savoring
10 minutes

Choose one value this good feeling connects to. Stand in a posture that physically represents it, not performed, genuinely felt, and breathe from it for the full ten minutes. Positive states fade fast unless the body is given time to register them.

Guided savoring practice, Tara Talks

These videos are made by outside teachers, not innerbalance101. They open in a new tab on YouTube.

C · Go deeper into the thought

A thought creates an emotion. An emotion creates a feeling in the body. A feeling drives an action. Most of what you do today started as a thought you never questioned. Here's what that chain can actually look like, and what changes when you meet the thought with curiosity instead of belief.

The old pattern
Thought
“I’m fat.”
→
Feeling
Heavy in the chest and stomach.
→
Action
Dresses to hide instead of how she actually wants to. Plays smaller. Less confident.
✦ Curiosity + Self-Trust + Beginner’s Mind: Where is this thought actually coming from? Who taught me my body needed to look a certain way to be worth something?
The new pattern
Thought
“What if my body is just a body, moving me through what I need, carrying me?”
→
Feeling
Something in the chest loosens.
→
Action
“How can I nurture it?” Dresses for herself. Movement becomes care instead of performance.

The work isn't forcing a positive thought on top of the old one. It's asking where the old thought actually came from, and finding out if it's still true for the life you're building. Return to the thought you named in Part One. Meet it with these seven postures. Move through as many as you need, you don't have to use all seven every day.

Curiosity

Approach the thought like you are encountering it for the first time, not like you already know what it means about you.

What is this thought actually trying to tell me, underneath what it sounds like?

No Judgment

Notice the thought without deciding whether having it makes you weak, dramatic, or behind.

What would I notice here if I were not also judging myself for noticing it?

Acceptance

This thought is already here. Fighting its presence costs more than the thought itself does.

What changes if I stop trying to not have this thought?

Beginner’s Mind

Set aside what you think you already know about yourself and this pattern. Look at what is actually here today.

If I had never worked on this before, what would I notice for the first time?

Self-Trust

You are allowed to trust what you are sensing, even before you can fully explain it.

What do I already know here that I have been talking myself out of?

Gratitude

Not for the discomfort itself, but for what it is protecting or pointing to.

What is this thought trying to keep me safe from, and is that protection still needed?

Generosity

Extend to yourself the same read you would give someone you love who was carrying this exact thought.

What would I say to someone I love who told me this thought, word for word?

“The belief was adopted to stay safe, loved, or successful once. The question now is whether it still serves the life you are building.”

The belief underneath the thought

Where did I first learn this was how I needed to be? Is it still true for the life I am building now? What would I choose to believe instead?

03
Part Three · Move Onward
Integrate & Affirm
5 minutes

You're not adding anything new here. You're taking what Part Two surfaced and deciding, on purpose, to carry it forward instead of letting it fade the moment you stand up.

The thought you're choosing instead
Seal it

Pick the one your body can actually receive right now. Not the most impressive. The most true.

  • “I don’t need to have all the answers right now. I’m on the right path, at the exact moment I’m meant to be in.”
  • “I trust what I already know, and that feels amazing.”
  • “Slowing down is living, and this life is just that.”
  • “I can hold this sense of inner peace, and come back to it again and again.”
  • “My worth is mine, and it feels amazing.”
  • “I have built something real. I can feel it, and that makes it real.”

Then smile. A real, physical smile, for having given yourself these minutes.

After 21 days

The practice is finished teaching you.
Now it is just yours.

By the end of these 21 days, you're not learning the shape of this practice anymore. You know it. Feel, transform, move onward. Three parts, and you decide how long each one takes on a given day.

That's the whole system, complete. Three stages, three 21-day practices, one shape that doesn't change. What you bring to it will.

System complete

You built an inner world.
Now comes the work of keeping it.

Most people never build what you have built. They manage their emotional states from the outside, through wins, approval, and activity, and they never stop long enough to know what is happening on the inside. You did the other thing.

What you do not maintain returns to its default. High alert is the nervous system’s natural setting when nothing is being done to override it. Inner balance is not a destination you arrive at. It is the discipline that overrides the default, every day.

What maintains it is the practice itself, now yours to keep. When a familiar state activates, the kind that costs you when it goes unattended, you return to the three parts and move through it. Feel it. Transform it. Move onward. Nothing to construct. You already know the way, and you decide how long you need each time you come back to it.
