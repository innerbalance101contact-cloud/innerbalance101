What this is

Stage 2 gave you the tools.
Now you learn when to use them.

In Stage 2 you built the noticing. You can feel what is here, name it, and catch the thought that comes with it, even on a full day.

Naming it is powerful, and for a lot of people it is also where they stop. Having a toolbox does not mean you know which tool the moment needs. Having a wrench does not mean you know when to pull it out.

Here, you put it together. You have 30 minutes, in two parts. Reset moves what you are carrying through your body. Inner Compass follows the thought back to the belief running it, so you can choose what you build from. Consistency is the key. Mindfulness is a daily practice, and more than once a day is ideal.

Every day is different. What you find on Monday will not be what you find on Thursday. The order stays the same. What you bring to it changes.

Before you begin. Part 2 asks for real thinking, not quick answers. Give each step the time it asks for. Not the time on the clock.

When to do it
  • Morning. Before the day’s demands arrive.
  • Between. One task ends, the next has not begun.
  • Evening. Close what the day opened.
What you need
  • A timer or stopwatch. These thirty minutes are yours, start to finish.
  • A quiet space. Somewhere you feel safe, where nobody needs you for thirty minutes.
  • A notebook and pen. Or loose paper. You will write in both parts.
  • Optional. Some music, an essential oil, a candle. Pick one thing that tells your body this time is for you.
How each day works

Two parts, in this order. Same every day. Do not skip ahead.

Why this order. Reset lets what you feel move through. Inner Compass shows you the loop that keeps it coming back, and breaks it at the belief. Letting it be, without change, gives you the same results. You need both.

Part 1: Reset
10 minutes, voice-guided. Follow along with the steps below.

The practice you already know. Feel it, catch the thought, release it. Ten minutes to clear the day.

A. Feel it (3 min)

Sit with both feet on the floor. Scan slowly from your feet to the top of your head. Notice where there is tension, weight, heat, numbness or constriction. Then tap every word on the wheel that is true for you right now.

This is acceptance. Let it be here. No judgment, no fixing.

Surprised Fearful Angry Disgusted Sad Happy

Tap a word to mark it. Tap again to remove it.

What you’re carrying today

Let each one be here for a few breaths. They’re allowed to be here.

B. Catch the thought (2 min)

What thought is running underneath what you just named? Write the exact sentence, not the edited version. One or two lines. No fixing yet.

Write it in a notebook or your notes app, or for lines to write on.

C. Release it (5 min)

Brought back to the six core feelings

This tells you which movement to use below.

Match the practice to whatever you marked. If you marked more than one family, start with whichever has the most charge right now. If you are not sure, tap. Set a timer for the full time and stay in it. The nervous system needs time, not effort.

Feet hip-width apart, knees soft, let the body vibrate gently from the ground up, increasing if it wants to. This is how the nervous system discharges what being let down, humiliated, or made furious leaves behind.

Inhale through the nose for four counts, exhale through the mouth for eight with an audible sigh, for about three minutes. Then wash your hands and face in cool water, slowly, noticing the sensation the whole time. Disgust wants something out of the body. Give it a physical exit.

02
Part 2 of 2: Inner Compass

Break the loop at the belief

20 minutes

Letting it be is not enough. Left alone, the same belief keeps producing the same outcome.

A belief creates a thought. The thought creates a feeling. The feeling drives an action. The action creates an outcome. Then the outcome becomes proof, and feeds the belief. That is why the same pattern runs for years. Break the belief, and you get a new outcome.

Start with how you show up. For driven, high-performing women, work is where this loop runs loudest. The same recipe works in every other part of your life. Once you know it, take it there.

Look at what you find the way you would look at someone you love. Curious, not critical. That is the lens for every step below.

Example: Lunch at the desk

Small, almost invisible, and most of us have done it. Here is the loop running, and the same moment after the belief changes.

TodayNew path
Belief

Working through lunch is productive.

A real break makes my afternoon better.

Thought

I don’t have time to stop. Stopping means falling behind.

Stepping away is part of how I do good work.

Feeling

A low hum of pressure. A braced chest.

Steadier. A little more room.

Action

Eats at the desk, or skips it, and keeps going.

Takes lunch away from the desk.

Outcome

The list gets done. A small hit of “done.” Foggy by three, but it still counts as proof.

The afternoon goes fine, maybe better. That is the first evidence for the new belief.

B. Question it (5 min)

The belief was adopted to keep you safe, loved or successful. It did a job. Now find out whether it is still true, or only familiar. Meet it with each of these postures. Take what is useful and leave the rest.

Curiosity

Approach the thought like you are encountering it for the first time, not like you already know what it means about you.

What is this thought actually trying to tell me, underneath what it sounds like?

No Judgment

Notice the thought without deciding whether having it makes you weak, dramatic, or behind.

What would I notice here if I were not also judging myself for noticing it?

Acceptance

This thought is already here. Fighting its presence costs more than the thought itself does.

What changes if I stop trying to not have this thought?

Beginner’s Mind

Set aside what you think you already know about yourself and this pattern. Look at what is actually here today.

If I had never worked on this before, what would I notice for the first time?

Self-Trust

You are allowed to trust what you are sensing, even before you can fully explain it.

What do I already know here that I have been talking myself out of?

Gratitude

Not for the discomfort itself, but for what it is protecting or pointing to.

What is this thought trying to keep me safe from, and is that protection still needed?

Generosity

Extend to yourself the same read you would give someone you love who was carrying this exact thought.

What would I say to someone I love who told me this thought, word for word?

Is it true, or just familiar? Do I want to keep living from it?

C. Choose it (4 min)

A belief does not change because you repeat a better sentence. It changes when the new one is believable and your life gives it evidence. Three moves.

1Write itA belief you can accept today. Gentler and truer, not forced. A bridge works: “I am learning that a real break helps my work.”
2Rate itHow believable, from 0 to 10? Aim for 5 or more. Below that, soften the words.
3Prove itName one time the old belief was not true. A day you rested and nothing fell apart.

How an experience gets built.
And how you change it.

Nothing reaches you raw. Something happens, and before you have decided anything about it, it passes through what you believe. That filtered version becomes an emotion, then a feeling in your body, then a thought, then an action. Repeat the action enough and it becomes a habit.

Here is the cycle, and what it looks like in a moment a lot of people know.

Enter this cycle anywhere. That entry point is the practice.

StimulusSomething reaches you
BeliefFilters it
EmotionWhat gets generated
FeelingIn the body
ThoughtWhat you tell yourself
ActionWhat you do

Repeat the action enough times and it becomes a habit. And the habit is what decides how you meet the next thing that reaches you.

What beliefs do

The belief is the filter.
Most of what you do runs through it.

A belief is an internal rule you picked up long ago about how you need to be to stay safe, loved or successful. It was useful then. It kept you in the room. Now it runs quietly in the background, shaping the thoughts you think and the actions you take, without ever asking whether it still fits.

That is why you can do everything right and still feel the same weight. The thought and the action are the visible part. The belief is what keeps producing them.

Acceptance and change

Accepting is not resigning.
You need both.

Acceptance means you stop fighting what you feel. You let it be here, without judgment. That is how an emotion starts to move.

Resignation is different. It says, this is just how I am, and nothing changes. It accepts the feeling and leaves the cause alone.

If the belief stays the same, the thoughts stay the same, and the same emotions come back tomorrow. To change how you feel, and how you live, you have to look at the beliefs and the thoughts they create.

Reset is acceptance. Inner Compass is change.

What you can do about it

You can change it once you can see it.

Seeing the belief does not mean fighting it. It means finding out where it came from and deciding whether it belongs in the life you are building. Then you choose a new one, and you act from it. That is building from intention. It is how you start living by choice instead of by habit.

How it fits together

Three skills.
One practice.

Between what reaches you and what you do, there is a moment. Each skill works inside it.

  • Mindfulness is noticing what is happening, on purpose and without judgment. It opens the moment.
  • Emotional transformation is what you do with the moment: clear what is here, then question the belief behind it so the emotion changes instead of running you.
  • Intuition is what you hear when things settle. You already know more than the noise lets you admit.
