Come back to yourself.

A short guide for the version of you that has been quietly changing since 2020, and the practices that help you trust what you already know.

Where this came from

I have spent a long time thinking about what changed in me since 2020. Some of it was loss. I will not pretend otherwise.

But some of it, quietly, was gain. I let go of a version of success I had built without ever asking if it was mine. I questioned things I had never let myself question before. Somewhere in the middle of that, I learned to trust myself again, not because someone told me to, but because I finally gave myself the room to.

I still have hard days. I am not writing this from the other side of it. I am writing this from inside it, grateful for what it has taught me so far.

If you are somewhere in that same middle right now, questioning what you built or wondering if the version of you the world sees is still the version you want to be, this is for you.

Why this stage isn't a mistake

The psychologist Carl Jung believed the first half of life is spent building an outer life. A career. An identity. The roles we think we're supposed to play.

Later, sometimes around midlife, some people get the chance to turn inward and ask who they are underneath all of that. It isn't guaranteed. Not everyone hits that turn at 40. Some never do.

What you can do is build the conditions to notice it when it comes. A repeatable practice. A way to pause, listen, and choose based on what's real instead of what you think you're supposed to be.

That steady loop is what rebuilds self-trust and intuition. It isn't about becoming someone else. It's about becoming more fully who you already are.

Three practices to start with

Mindfulness for the questioning stage

Not fixes. Practices you return to.

01
Self-trust
The two minute check-in

Before you answer a question that was never yours to answer for someone else, stop. Give yourself two minutes. Ask what you actually think, before you think about what you're supposed to think. Write one sentence. Don't edit it.

02
Questioning old beliefs
Whose voice is this

Pick one rule you've been living by. Be more. Achieve more. Don't slow down. Ask where it came from. A parent. A boss. A culture you absorbed before you were old enough to question it. Then ask if it's still true for you, or if it's just old.

03
Sitting with discomfort
Name it, don't fix it

On a hard day, don't reach for the fix first. Set a timer for five minutes. Name what you feel in one word. Let it sit there without doing anything about it yet. The urge to fix it fast is the exact pattern you're unlearning.

Three prompts for when you feel misaligned

Use these when the path you're on stops feeling like yours.

01
What version of success am I still chasing that was never actually mine?
02
If I stopped proving myself for one week, what would I do differently?
03
What is the quiet thought I keep pushing down because it's inconvenient right now?
