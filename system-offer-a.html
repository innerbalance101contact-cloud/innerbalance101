What I had wrong

None of it was wrong.
I just didn't know which one to reach for.

I want to be clear about this, because it took me years to understand it. All of that helped me. It still helps me. The yoga, the meditation, the reiki, the thought work, the books, the podcasts, the counselling. None of it was a waste.

A lot of people talk about having a toolbox. But having a toolbox doesn't mean you know how to use each one for a specific job. Having a wrench doesn't mean you know when to pull it out.

You have to work out how you're actually feeling in that moment, first, in order to know what tool you need. That was the part nobody was teaching me.

So I'd reach for whatever I'd read about most recently. Sometimes it fit. Most of the time it was the wrong tool for that particular day, and I'd quietly decide the tool was the problem.

And I couldn't read myself at all.

We consume so much information and never apply it. You have probably read more about your nervous system than most practitioners have. It sits in your head, filed, useful in a conversation.

We study the psychology of our emotions and still can't name what we're feeling while it's happening. You can explain your own patterns in detail afterwards. At three in the afternoon, in the meeting, in the middle of it, you've got nothing.

That noticing is a muscle. It's the window into the thoughts you can't see while you're on autopilot. Once you can name what you're feeling, you can pick the right thing for it. Until then you're guessing.

Why it works

The mechanism,
in plain terms.

Why the body first

Chronic stress takes the prefrontal cortex offline, and that is the part responsible for clear judgment and decision making. The somatic practices complete the stress response cycle your body has been stuck in, so those functions come back online.

Why thought work

An unexamined thought is a closed loop. It generates an emotion, which generates a behaviour, which confirms the thought. The thought work interrupts that loop. Not by swapping in a positive thought, but by creating enough space between the thought and the response that choice becomes possible.

Why 21 days

Days 1 to 7 create the pathway and it feels effortful, which is evidence the work is active. Days 8 to 14 stabilise it. Days 15 to 21 anchor it, and what was a discipline starts becoming a baseline. Twenty one days is the minimum window for that to begin.
