Why this is built the way it is.

The nervous system, thought work and the 21-day structure each have a reason behind them.

Chronic stress takes the clear-thinking part of the brain offline.Why the nervous system

Chronic stress takes the prefrontal cortex offline. This is the part of the brain responsible for clear judgment and decision-making. The somatic practices in this system are designed to complete the stress response cycle the body has been stuck in, so those functions come back online.

An unexamined thought is a closed loop.Why thought work

An unexamined thought generates an emotion, which generates a behaviour, which confirms the thought. The thought work in this system interrupts that loop. Not by replacing the thought with a positive one. By creating enough space between the thought and the response that choice becomes possible.

The nervous system changes through repetition, not through reading.Why 21 days

The nervous system does not change because you read something useful. It changes through repeated activation of new neural pathways until the brain begins to route automatically through them, rather than defaulting back to the old ones.

21 days is where the process begins, not where it completes. In one University College London study of everyday habits, the average was 66 days, and individuals ranged from 18 to 254. Each stage builds on what the previous one installed, so you cannot get to Stage 3 without having done Stage 1.

Days 1 to 7, create the pathway. You are doing something new. The brain is working harder than it will later. It does not feel automatic yet. That is normal. It is evidence the work is active.

Days 8 to 14, consolidate it. The pattern begins to stabilise. The practice starts to feel familiar and the body begins to know what comes next.

Days 15 to 21, anchor it. What was effortful becomes practiced. The brain has begun to route differently. This is when the change stops being a discipline and starts being a baseline.
